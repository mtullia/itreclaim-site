Data Sanitization & Destruction

Wiping or physical destruction, chosen for each type of drive and device, with a record for every asset when the work is done.

Wipe or Destroy, Chosen for Each Device

There is no single right method. The choice depends on the media, on whether the device will be reused, and on your own requirements.

  • Sanitization (Wiping)

    Data is removed with a verified process so the device can be safely resold or reused. This keeps value in working laptops, servers and drives.

    • Suited to working drives and devices with resale value
    • Each device is checked after the process
    • Failures are flagged and sent for destruction

  • Physical Destruction

    The media is destroyed so it cannot be read again. This is the route for failed drives, media that cannot be wiped, or any case where your policy calls for it.

    • Suited to damaged, failed or end-of-life media
    • Required where your policy rules out reuse
    • Destroyed items are recorded by serial number where readable

How the Method Is Chosen

  • Media Type

    Hard drives, solid-state drives, phones and tapes each need different handling to remove data reliably.

  • Your Requirements

    Internal policies, contracts and industry rules can require a specific method, and they always take priority.

  • Reuse or Resale

    Devices that can be resold are wiped where your rules allow it, so their value is not lost to destruction.

  • Recognized Guidance

    Methods are selected with reference to NIST SP 800-88 Rev. 2 and verified per project.

Data Security Questions

What happens if a drive will not wipe?

It is recorded as an exception and is not resold. The next step, usually physical destruction, follows the scope agreed for your project, and the record shows the outcome.

Can we keep our own list of serial numbers to compare?

Yes, and we encourage it. Send your list before pickup and the completion records can be checked against it item by item.

Can data be destroyed at our site, or only off site?

Options are confirmed per project; we tell you which are available for your site.

Which standard do you follow?

We follow the guidance named under How the Method Is Chosen, together with media-specific standards and any requirements in your contract.

When do we receive the certificate?

After the work is finished and checked. We do not issue certificates for work that has not been completed.

Have Drives or Devices That Hold Data?

Tell us what media is involved and any internal rules you follow. We will match the method to both.
